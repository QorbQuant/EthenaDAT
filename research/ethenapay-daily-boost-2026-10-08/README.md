# EthenaPay Daily Boost, accrual days September 1 to October 8, 2026

These files reproduce every computed figure in the review of what EthenaPay's Daily Boost paid. The review reads every USDe payment that the two reward accounts made to EthenaPay wallets on Avalanche C-Chain through the end of October 9, 2026 UTC, sets each payment for the accrual days of September 1 to October 8 against the wallet's time-weighted balance, and compares the result with the rates, caps and card condition in Ethena Pay's FAQ, Terms of Use and pricing page, which the review read on October 10, 2026 UTC.

No figure here is a forecast. Every number describes payments, balances and card payments recorded on Avalanche C-Chain by the end of October 9, 2026 UTC.

## Reproduce

From this directory, with Python 3 and the standard library only.

    python3 ethenapay_boost_review.py
    python3 make_chart_svg.py
    python3 check_article_numbers.py

The first script reads the inputs and runs its checks. If any check fails it stops before writing anything, so `out/` keeps the tables of the last clean run. Otherwise it rewrites the tables in `out/`. It confirms every input against `inputs/manifest.csv`, the chain files against `inputs/chain_manifest.csv` and the SHA-256 of `boost_recount.cjs`, and the chain read against the recount this site published with its EthenaPay adoption review. Every wallet's first USDe and its balance at block 96,815,525 must match that recount's `wallet_activity.csv`, and the daily flows and USDe held on all 144 days from May 15 to October 5 must match the daily file published with the cash-flow review, `daily_chain_62503a1.csv`, to the wei. It then rebuilds the matched wallet-days for October 1 and October 8 from the wallet files and the month totals from `month_wallets.csv`, and checks them against the payment periods. The matched counts for the other days of the window come from `periods.csv` as `boost_recount.cjs` wrote it. The second script draws the chart from `out/chart_points.csv` and `out/chart_lines.csv`. The third recomputes the article's computed figures, those in its metadata and the chart's text with separately written code and exits with an error if a phrase no longer appears. None of them uses the network.

`boost_recount.cjs` produced the files in `inputs/chain/`. It reads the Routescan explorer API for Avalanche C-Chain. It ran in a browser, on a page of the explorer API, as `EPB.run(fetch.bind(window), console.log)`, from 01:32:53 to 01:33:23 UTC on October 10, 2026, and returned the files as text. It runs the same way with Node 18 or later as `node boost_recount.cjs`, which writes them to `inputs/chain/`. Its synthetic test runs with `node test_boost_recount.cjs boost_recount.cjs` and needs no network. A rerun gives the same data files, and a `meta.json` that differs only in its run times, unless the explorer's history of these blocks changes. An earlier run at 01:17:59 UTC, before the script wrote `month_wallets.csv`, returned the same bytes for the six other data files.

## Columns

Amounts ending in `_wei` are USDe in wei, 10^18 to one USDe, and a time-weighted balance summed over days is in wei-days. The wallet-day files give time-weighted balances in millionths of USDe, rounded down, in the columns ending in `_micro`. Card payments are counted as transfers to a settlement address.

`periods.csv`. `payer` is base or topup, and topup covers the Boost account and the pilot account, which paid for no day from September 1. `period` numbers each account's periods from 0. `first_day`, `last_day` and `days` give the accrual days covered, `batches` the payment batches, `first_payment_utc` and `last_payment_utc` the first and last transfer, and `transfers`, `transactions`, `recipients` and `paid_wei` what was paid. `funded_wallets` counts the wallets with a balance over those days and `twa_wei_days` sums their time-weighted balances. `paid_wallets` counts the funded wallets paid and `paid_wallets_without_balance` the wallets paid with no balance over those days. `tested_wallets` counts the wallets with 100 USDe or more on average and `tested_wallets_without_other_flows` those of them with no other USDe in or out over the period. `fit_utc`, `fit_cet` and `fit_cest` count the tested wallets whose payment matched a rate within 1% on balances over UTC days and over days starting at 23:00 and 22:00 UTC, and `fit_utc_within_5pct` the same on UTC days within 5%. Each rate's column, from `base_4_5` to `six_2_0_cap_100k`, counts the tested wallets whose payment it matched first within 1% on UTC days, and is blank for the other account's rates. `unpaid` counts the tested wallets not paid, `other` those paid at no rate and `other_without_flows` those of them with no other USDe in or out. `median_rate_simple_pct` is the median tested wallet's payment over its time-weighted balance, times 365, in percent, with unpaid wallets counted at zero.

`activity.csv`. Rows for each top-up period by `scope` and `class`. `scope` is all for every wallet with a balance over the period and ge100 for the tested wallets. `class` groups wallets by card payments up to the end of the period's first day. A had one in that calendar month, B none in it and one in the month before, C only earlier ones, and D none. `wallets`, `paid_wallets` and `model_wallets` count the wallets, the paid ones and, for ge100, those matched at a rate within 1%. `twa_wei_days` and `paid_twa_wei_days` sum the time-weighted balances of all of them and of the paid ones, and `topup_wei` is the top-up paid.

`month_wallets.csv`. `twa_wei_days` sums the wallet's time-weighted balance over the month's accrual days. `tested_days` counts the days of the top-up periods in which it held 100 USDe or more on average, and `paid_days` the days of the top-up periods it was paid for. `topup_wei` is its top-up for the month's days, `topup_tested_wei` the part for its tested periods and `base_wei` its base payments for the month's days. The three card columns count its card payments before the month, in it and after it to October 9. `wallets_exchanged_with` counts the other wallets it exchanged USDe with and `of_which_spent_in_month` those of them with a card payment in the month.

`wallet_day_2026-10-01.csv` and `wallet_day_2026-10-08.csv`. One row per wallet with a balance or a payment for the day. `twa_micro`, `twa_cet_micro` and `twa_cest_micro` are its time-weighted balance over the UTC day and over the days starting at 23:00 and 22:00 UTC. `base_wei` and `topup_wei` are its two payments for the day, and `other_flows` is 1 if any other USDe moved in or out during the UTC day. The three card columns count its card payments up to the end of the accrual day, in that calendar month, in the month before and earlier. A wallet whose first card payment came after the accrual day shows none in these files, while `month_wallets.csv` counts card payments to October 9.

`batches.csv`. One row per batch of each reward account from June 9. `payer` is as in `periods.csv`, so the pilot account's batches, all before September, show as topup. `first_utc`, `last_utc`, `transfers`, `transactions`, `recipients` and `paid_wei` describe the batch, and `first_day` and `last_day` give the accrual days it covers, blank for a batch that paid for days before September 1.

`utc_days_after_recount.csv`. One row per UTC day from October 6 to 9 with the flows the published recount defines, deposits, withdrawals, reversals, the two reward accounts' payments, card payments and transfers between wallets, and `held_wei`, the USDe in wallets at the end of the day.

## Names and terms

A wallet is a smart-contract account that one of EthenaPay's two factory contracts created with CREATE2 on Avalanche C-Chain. It appears in these files as a position number in creation order, the numbering of the published recount, never as an address, and one wallet is not necessarily one person. The base account, `0xd0ec8cc7414f27ce85f8dece6b4a58225f273311`, is the reward account the dashboard's query labels yield. The top-up account, `0xab2b06efa6179e624f5e3b08b64978df114ff24f`, is the one the query labels other rewards. The article calls it the Boost account because its payments match the Daily Boost. The dashboard repository's notes describe the base account as the funding Safe of a Merkl campaign, which wallets claim from through the Merkl Distributor. The review counts every USDe transfer from a reward account to a wallet as a payment, whatever contract the transaction called. A third account the query labels other rewards, `0xc9dd0d4351d3841a712f59cb4f1e88c3d500515b`, made its 150 payments before September. These, the two factories, the two settlement addresses and USDe, in `boost_recount.cjs` and `inputs/chain/meta.json`, and the three infrastructure contracts that the factories created with a plain CREATE, in `inputs/recount/meta.json`, are the only addresses in these files. `boost_recount.cjs` also holds the AllowanceSpent topic, a hash and not an address.

A card payment is a USDe transfer from a wallet to the current or the retired settlement address, the flow the cash-flow review calls card spend. The site's spend events count AllowanceSpent logs instead. For every wallet in `month_wallets.csv`, the main script checks that both give the same answer on card use by September 30, and that a wallet with no card payment through October 9 had no spend event in the recount, which runs to October 5. The recount found one more transfer than spend events through October 5, a 5 USDe transfer on September 24 with no spend event, so a wallet whose only card payment was that transfer would count here as having used the card. Every card debit log to either settlement address through October 9 was in USDe, 34,156 and 836 logs.

An accrual day runs from midnight to midnight UTC. A wallet's time-weighted balance for a day is its USDe balance averaged over every second of the day, rewards included. A payment period is the set of accrual days that one or more payment batches cover. A batch is a run of transfers from one account with no gap of more than three hours. A period starts the day after the last day the account's previous period covered and ends on the last day that ended before its first batch. A tested wallet holds 100 USDe or more on average over the days of a period. A rate's daily share, `(1 + rate) ^ (1/365) - 1`, is the daily rate that compounds to the yearly rate over 365 days. A payment matches a rate when it comes within 1% of the time-weighted balance up to the cap times the rate's daily share, and the review tries the rates in a fixed order and takes the first that matches. For the groups of October 1 and 8, a wallet that matches no rate within 1% goes under the first rate it matches within 5%. The rates are 4.5% and 4.0% for the base account, and for the top-up account 0.5% and 1.0% to $5,000, 1.5% and 2.0% to $1,000,000, and 1.5% and 2.0% to $100,000. A yearly rate before compounding is the payment divided by the balance, times 365, and the rate after compounding is the daily rate compounded over 365 days.

A calendar month runs from midnight UTC on its first day. In `month_wallets.csv` a wallet's card payments in the month count every card payment in it, for October up to the end of October 9, and its card payments after the month run from the month's end to October 9. Two wallets exchanged USDe if either sent USDe to the other at any time to October 9.

## How this review read its sources

The browser read the explorer API from its own page, as the published recount did. Earlier versions of the script ran from 00:59:53 UTC on October 10, 2026, the last of them from 01:17:59 to 01:19:20 UTC, and the run whose files these are from 01:32:53 to 01:33:23 UTC. The block times in `meta.json` place block 97,161,009 at 23:59:59 UTC on October 9 and the next block at 00:00:01 UTC on October 10. The browser read Ethena Pay's FAQ from 00:28:50 to 00:29:04 UTC, the Terms of Use from 00:29:15 to 00:29:30 UTC and the pricing page at 01:14:55 UTC, all on October 10, and `inputs/terms_facts.csv` gives the facts taken from them in paraphrase. The recount files and the cash-flow review's results came from the site's repository at commit 70e4e45, copied at about 00:47 UTC on October 10, byte-identical to the reviews' downloads. The browser downloaded nothing to disk.

## Limits

The chain shows wallets, and the terms set the card condition for a user, so a person could use the card from one wallet and keep USDe in another. The files count how many wallets exchanged USDe with another wallet, which catches a direct transfer between two wallets and not one owner funding both from the same outside account. The terms let Ethena Pay specify other qualifying activity, and no page read for this review says what it is. The Terms of Use read on October 10 were last updated on September 22 and the pricing page on September 30, so this review did not see the wording in force earlier in September. As in the site's other reviews, USDe sent through a wallet's card allowance to two unidentified addresses counts as a withdrawal. Through October 5, one wallet that sent USDe to the address in use after June 10 had never paid a settlement address. The chain does not show a wallet's tier, so the 6% group is the wallets paid at 1.5%, and only a balance above $100,000 shows whether the Pro or the VIP cap applied. The accrual days end on October 8 and the card payments on October 9, so October was incomplete. A rate is matched within 1%. That separates the daily share from the same rate divided by 365 for the base payments, 4.40% against 4.5% a year before compounding, but not for the Boost, 0.499% against 0.5%. For the Boost the capped Standard payment of 0.06832295325093 USDe, within 0.000000000000002 of 5,000 times the daily share of 0.5%, and the median payments carry that point. Most payments that matched no rate went to wallets whose USDe moved in or out during the day.
